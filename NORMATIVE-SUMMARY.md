# Normative summary: Context Layer v0.2

**One-page reading guide, not a substitute for the specification.** Applies to `context-layer/0.2-draft` and the profiles it names. This is an informative topic map; preserve each source requirement's conditions and strength when implementing it. The [complete requirement index](NORMATIVE-INDEX.md) reproduces every core uppercase MUST/SHOULD passage, including governed lists and tables, with direct source links.

- **Authority and disclosure:** Consumers receive approved context, with an authenticated requester, purpose, recipient, scope, and validity window. Preserve source permissions and provenance; allow scope reduction; never infer absent permissions. [§2.2–3](protocol/spec/context-layer-technical-specification.md#cl-s-2-2), [§4.1](protocol/spec/context-layer-technical-specification.md#cl-s-4-1)
- **Representation and integrity:** Validate required envelope fields, timestamps, versions, and closed schemas. Keep identifiers private. A deployment chooses canonicalization and key verification before claiming cryptographic verifiability. [§6](protocol/spec/context-layer-technical-specification.md#cl-s-6)
- **Objects and lifecycles:** Preserve native security metadata and contradictory claims. Match receipt level to its required flag; authorize exact purpose codes. Bind decisions to policy snapshots; keep credentials out of bundles and secrets out of receipts. Respect expiry and retention; preserve proposal audit history where required. Changed disclosure requests need a new decision. [§7–8](protocol/spec/context-layer-technical-specification.md#cl-s-7)
- **Policy and approval:** Evaluate all listed policy inputs, record variable decision signals, apply the deny-by-default recommendations, and show every listed approval detail. Bind approval to the request; isolate writeback as a proposal. [§9](protocol/spec/context-layer-technical-specification.md#cl-s-9)
- **HTTP and Lite:** The optional HTTP binding adds transport, authentication, credential, version, idempotency, and safe-error rules. Lite's eight obligations include all five object contracts, four decision outcomes, finite expiry, single use, binding, vault isolation, proposal-only writeback, and receipts. [§10](protocol/spec/context-layer-technical-specification.md#cl-s-10)
- **Security and privacy:** Separate authentication from authorization, protect credentials, minimize data, isolate untrusted content, test transformations, limit discovery inference, state revocation limits, and govern receipts independently. Fail closed when required services are unavailable; an irreversible side effect with a missing completion receipt is indeterminate. [§11](protocol/spec/context-layer-technical-specification.md#cl-s-11)
- **Interop and evidence:** Follow every adapter and consumer rule. Publish applicable conformance results; preserve version compatibility and migration constraints. Synthetic integrations are not production integrations. [§12–16](protocol/spec/context-layer-technical-specification.md#cl-s-12)

**Counting and scope:** The core has 123 raw keyword occurrences: 98 MUST and 25 SHOULD, including their negative forms. Four occur in the BCP 14 definition; 119 remain. These are occurrences, not 119 distinct requirements: one keyword can govern many bullets, and one passage can contain several obligations. The reported figure of 371 is not reproduced for this core. Separate companion and Nostr drafts are outside this index; see its [scope inventory](NORMATIVE-INDEX.md#scope-and-counting).

**Role scope:** §13 explicitly summarizes role responsibilities without independently imposing requirements. All 37 role bullets remain visible in the index supplement. Existing uppercase obligations, conditions & profile applicability remain unchanged; see the [compatibility note](CHANGELOG.md) for earlier stricter readings. Full implementation still requires the source, schemas & applicable profile tests.
